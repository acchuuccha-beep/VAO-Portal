## SET 1 (Original 30 Questions)

### Easy (17)

**Q1. The Governor of a State is appointed by whom?**
(1) Chief Minister of the State
(2) President of India
(3) Prime Minister of India
(4) Chief Justice of the High Court

**Answer:** (2) President of India
**Explanation:** Article 155 states that the Governor is appointed by the President by warrant under his hand and seal. The Governor is a nominee of the Central Government, not elected by the people.
**Article:** 155

---

**Q2. The Governor holds office for a term of 5 years, but:**
(1) Can only be removed by a constitutional amendment
(2) Can be removed earlier by the Chief Minister
(3) Holds office during the pleasure of the President and can be removed earlier
(4) Must always complete the full 5-year term

**Answer:** (3) Holds office during the pleasure of the President and can be removed earlier
**Explanation:** Under Article 156, the Governor holds office during the pleasure of the President. There is no impeachment process for the Governor; he can be removed by the President at any time without assigning reasons.
**Article:** 156

---

**Q3. Which of the following is a qualification for appointment as Governor of a State?**
(1) Must be a native of that State
(2) Must be 25 years of age
(3) Must be a citizen of India and at least 35 years of age
(4) Must have served in the judiciary

**Answer:** (3) Must be a citizen of India and at least 35 years of age
**Explanation:** Article 157 requires the Governor to be a citizen of India and at least 35 years old. There is NO requirement that the Governor belong to the State he is appointed to — in fact, convention dictates a Governor is usually from outside the State.
**Article:** 157

---

**Q4. Before whom does the Governor take the oath of office?**
(1) Chief Minister
(2) Chief Justice of the State High Court (or senior-most judge)
(3) President of India
(4) Speaker of the Legislative Assembly

**Answer:** (2) Chief Justice of the State High Court (or senior-most judge)
**Explanation:** Article 159 requires the Governor to take oath before the Chief Justice of the High Court of that State, or in his absence, the senior-most judge of that High Court available.
**Article:** 159

---

**Q6. Which Article states that the Council of Ministers shall aid and advise the Governor in the exercise of his functions?**
(1) Article 154
(2) Article 163
(3) Article 174
(4) Article 178

**Answer:** (2) Article 163
**Explanation:** Article 163 provides for a Council of Ministers headed by the Chief Minister to aid and advise the Governor. Exceptions: the Governor can act in his discretion in a few situations (e.g., Article 356).
**Article:** 163

---

**Q7. The Chief Minister of a State is appointed by:**
(1) President of India
(2) Governor of the State
(3) Speaker of the Legislative Assembly
(4) Chief Justice of the High Court

**Answer:** (2) Governor of the State
**Explanation:** Article 164(1) states that the Chief Minister is appointed by the Governor. The Governor invites the leader of the majority party (or coalition) in the Legislative Assembly to form the government.
**Article:** 164(1)

---

**Q8. The Council of Ministers in a State is collectively responsible to:**
(1) The Governor
(2) The President
(3) The State Legislative Assembly
(4) The State Legislative Council

**Answer:** (3) The State Legislative Assembly
**Explanation:** Article 164(2) makes the Council of Ministers collectively responsible to the Legislative Assembly of the State. The Council (and its Council) cannot survive without the confidence of the Assembly.
**Article:** 164(2)

---

**Q10. The oath of office and secrecy to the Chief Minister and Ministers is administered by:**
(1) President of India
(2) Speaker of the Assembly
(3) Governor of the State
(4) Chief Justice of the High Court

**Answer:** (3) Governor of the State
**Explanation:** Under Article 164(3), the CM and Ministers take oath of office and secrecy before the Governor. The format is specified in the Third Schedule.
**Article:** 164(3)

---

**Q12. In a bicameral State, the State Legislature consists of:**
(1) Governor + Assembly
(2) Governor + Assembly + Council
(3) Assembly + Council only
(4) Assembly only

**Answer:** (2) Governor + Assembly + Council
**Explanation:** Article 168 defines the State Legislature as the Governor + Legislative Assembly + Legislative Council (in bicameral states). The Governor is PART of the legislature for lawmaking purposes.
**Article:** 168

---

**Q14. The maximum strength of a State Legislative Assembly is:**
(1) 400 members
(2) 500 members
(3) 550 members
(4) 600 members

**Answer:** (2) 500 members
**Explanation:** Article 170 provides that the Assembly shall have not more than 500 and not less than 60 members. Karnataka has 224 members.
**Article:** 170

---

**Q15. The normal duration of a State Legislative Assembly is:**
(1) 4 years
(2) 5 years
(3) 6 years
(4) 3 years

**Answer:** (2) 5 years
**Explanation:** Article 172 provides a 5-year term for the Assembly from the date of its first meeting. It can be dissolved earlier by the Governor, and extended by Parliament (one year at a time) during a national emergency.
**Article:** 172

---

**Q16. The minimum age to become a member of the State Legislative Assembly is:**
(1) 21 years
(2) 25 years
(3) 30 years
(4) 35 years

**Answer:** (2) 25 years
**Explanation:** Article 173 sets the minimum age of 25 for Assembly membership. (For the Council, the minimum age is 30.)
**Article:** 173

---

**Q17. Who has the power to dissolve the State Legislative Assembly?**
(1) Chief Minister
(2) Speaker
(3) Governor
(4) Chief Justice of the High Court

**Answer:** (3) Governor
**Explanation:** Article 174(2)(b) gives the Governor power to dissolve the Legislative Assembly. This is done on the advice of the CM (unless a government has lost majority).
**Article:** 174(2)

---

**Q18. The Speaker and Deputy Speaker of the Legislative Assembly are elected by:**
(1) The Governor
(2) The Chief Minister
(3) The members of the Assembly
(4) The members of both houses

**Answer:** (3) The members of the Assembly
**Explanation:** Article 178 provides that the Speaker and Deputy Speaker are elected by the members of the Assembly from among themselves. The Speaker presides over Assembly sittings and has a casting vote.
**Article:** 178

---

**Q19. The Governor has the right to address the State Legislature and send messages under:**
(1) Article 170
(2) Article 175
(3) Article 200
(4) Article 213

**Answer:** (2) Article 175
**Explanation:** Article 175 gives the Governor the right to address either or both houses of the Legislature and to send messages. This is the counterpart of Article 86 for the President.
**Article:** 175

---

**Q23. A Money Bill in a State can be introduced ONLY in:**
(1) The Legislative Council
(2) The Legislative Assembly
(3) Either house
(4) A joint sitting

**Answer:** (2) The Legislative Assembly
**Explanation:** Article 198: Money Bills can only be introduced in the Assembly (not the Council). The Council can only make recommendations, which the Assembly may accept or reject.
**Article:** 198

---

**Q28. The Advocate General of a State is appointed by:**
(1) Chief Minister
(2) Governor
(3) Chief Justice of the High Court
(4) State Bar Council

**Answer:** (2) Governor
**Explanation:** Article 165: the Advocate General is the highest law officer of the State, appointed by the Governor. He must be qualified to be a High Court judge. He holds office during the pleasure of the Governor.
**Article:** 165

---

### Medium (11)

**Q5. The Governor can grant pardon for:**
(1) Offenses against laws on Union List matters
(2) Offenses against laws on State List matters
(3) Death sentences
(4) Court-martial sentences

**Answer:** (2) Offenses against laws on State List matters
**Explanation:** Under Article 161, the Governor can pardon, reprieve, respite, or remit punishments for offenses against State List laws. The Governor CANNOT pardon a death sentence or court-martial offenses — those are the exclusive powers of the President (Article 72).
**Article:** 161

---

**Q9. The 91st Constitutional Amendment Act, 2003, limits the size of the State Council of Ministers to:**
(1) 10% of the Assembly strength
(2) 15% of the Assembly strength
(3) 20% of the Assembly strength
(4) A fixed number of 25 members

**Answer:** (2) 15% of the Assembly strength
**Explanation:** Article 164(1A) caps the Council of Ministers (including the CM) at 15% of the total Assembly membership, with a minimum of 12. For Karnataka (224 seats), 15% = 33.6, i.e., max 34.
**Article:** 164(1A)

---

**Q11. The rules for the transaction of government business of the State are made by:**
(1) Chief Minister
(2) Governor
(3) State Legislature
(4) Advocate General

**Answer:** (2) Governor
**Explanation:** Article 166 gives the Governor power to make rules for the convenient transaction of government business and allocation of work among ministers.
**Article:** 166

---

**Q13. A Legislative Council can be created or abolished in a State:**
(1) By the State Legislature alone
(2) By Parliament by law, after a special-majority Assembly resolution
(3) By the Governor's ordinance
(4) By the President alone

**Answer:** (2) By Parliament by law, after a special-majority Assembly resolution
**Explanation:** Article 169 provides that Parliament may by law create or abolish a Legislative Council if the Assembly passes a resolution by a special majority (majority of total membership + 2/3 of members present and voting).
**Article:** 169

---

**Q20. The Governor's special address to the Legislature at the start of the first session of the year and after a general election is provided under:**
(1) Article 174
(2) Article 176
(3) Article 178
(4) Article 188

**Answer:** (2) Article 176
**Explanation:** Article 176 requires the Governor to address the Legislature at the commencement of the first session after a general election and the first session of each year. The address outlines the government's policy agenda.
**Article:** 176

---

**Q21. Under which Article does the Governor promulgate ordinances when the Legislature is not in session?**
(1) Article 123
(2) Article 200
(3) Article 213
(4) Article 165

**Answer:** (3) Article 213
**Explanation:** Article 213 empowers the Governor to promulgate ordinances when the State Legislature is not in session. (Article 123 is the corresponding provision for the President.) An ordinance must be laid before the Legislature within 6 weeks of reassembly.
**Article:** 213

---

**Q22. An ordinance issued by the Governor lapses:**
(1) After 1 month
(2) 6 weeks after the Legislature reassembles
(3) After 1 year
(4) Only when repealed by the Governor

**Answer:** (2) 6 weeks after the Legislature reassembles
**Explanation:** Article 213(2)(a): the ordinance ceases to operate 6 weeks after the Legislature reassembles, unless disapproved earlier or passed as an Act.
**Article:** 213

---

**Q24. The Legislative Council can delay a Money Bill for a maximum of:**
(1) 7 days
(2) 14 days
(3) 30 days
(4) 3 months

**Answer:** (2) 14 days
**Explanation:** The Council must return a Money Bill within 14 days; after that it is deemed passed. This mirrors the Rajya Sabha's 14-day limit on Money Bills.
**Article:** 198

---

**Q25. When a Bill is passed by the State Legislature, the Governor may:**
(1) Give assent
(2) Withhold assent
(3) Return the Bill for reconsideration
(4) Reserve the Bill for the President
Select the correct option:
(1) Only (1) and (2)
(2) Only (1), (2) and (3)
(3) (1), (2), (3) and (4)
(4) Only (1) and (4)

**Answer:** (3) (1), (2), (3) and (4)
**Explanation:** Article 200 gives the Governor four options: grant assent, withhold assent, return the Bill for reconsideration, or reserve the Bill for the President's consideration.
**Article:** 200

---

**Q29. Elections to the Panchayats and Municipalities in a State are conducted by:**
(1) Election Commission of India
(2) State Election Commission
(3) State Finance Commission
(4) District Magistrate

**Answer:** (2) State Election Commission
**Explanation:** Under Article 243K (Panchayats) and Article 243ZA (Municipalities), a State Election Commission conducts local body elections. The Election Commission of India conducts only Parliamentary and State Assembly elections.
**Articles:** 243K, 243ZA

---

**Q30. The State Finance Commission reviews the financial position of:**
(1) The State Government
(2) Panchayats and Municipalities
(3) State Public Service Commission
(4) High Court

**Answer:** (2) Panchayats and Municipalities
**Explanation:** Article 243I: the State Finance Commission reviews the financial position of Panchayats and Municipalities and recommends devolution of state funds to local bodies, once every 5 years.
**Article:** 243I

---

### Advanced (2)

**Q26. If the Governor returns a Bill for reconsideration and the Legislature passes it again, the Governor:**
(1) May withhold assent again
(2) Must grant assent
(3) Must reserve it for the President
(4) Can ignore the Bill

**Answer:** (2) Must grant assent
**Explanation:** Article 200: if the House passes the Bill again, with or without amendment, the Governor MUST grant assent. (Note: unlike the President under Article 111, the Governor has no veto over a re-passed Bill.)
**Article:** 200

---

**Q27. When a Bill is reserved by the Governor for the President, the President must decide on it within:**
(1) 2 weeks
(2) 1 month
(3) 6 months
(4) No time limit

**Answer:** (3) 6 months
**Explanation:** Article 201: the President can give assent, withhold assent, or return the Bill for reconsideration, and must act within 6 months of receiving it. Such Bills usually concern Union/Concurrent List matters.
**Article:** 201

## SET 2 (New 30 Questions)

### Medium (20)

**Q31. In which of the following situations does the Governor exercise DISCRETION (independent of the Council of Ministers' advice)?**
(1) Summoning the Legislature
(2) Appointing the Chief Minister when no party has a clear majority
(3) Returning a Bill for reconsideration
(4) Assenting to a Money Bill

**Answer:** (2) Appointing the Chief Minister when no party has a clear majority
**Explanation:** When no single party/collition has a clear majority, the Governor has discretion in inviting the leader who can command a majority in the Assembly. Other options are exercised on the aid and advice of the Council of Ministers.
**Article:** 163(1) read with 164(1)

---

**Q32. The executive power of a State extends to matters with respect to which the State Legislature has power to make laws, BUT it does NOT extend to:**
(1) Subjects in the State List
(2) Subjects in the Concurrent List over which Parliament or Union executive power is expressly reserved
(3) Subjects in the State List not specifically reserved
(4) Local government matters

**Answer:** (2) Subjects in the Concurrent List over which Parliament or Union executive power is expressly reserved
**Explanation:** Article 162 provides that the executive power of the State extends to matters in the State List, but for Concurrent List subjects the Union retains executive power where the Constitution or a Union law expressly provides so.
**Article:** 162

---

**Q33. Which Article requires the Chief Minister to communicate to the Governor all decisions of the Council of Ministers relating to the administration of the State?**
(1) Article 166
(2) Article 167
(3) Article 163
(4) Article 174

**Answer:** (2) Article 167
**Explanation:** Article 167 imposes a duty on the CM to furnish to the Governor information relating to administrative decisions and proposals for legislation. This ensures the Governor is kept informed even though he acts on advice.
**Article:** 167

---

**Q34. Ministers in a State hold office:**
(1) During the pleasure of the Chief Minister
(2) During the pleasure of the Governor
(3) For a fixed term of 5 years
(4) During the pleasure of the Assembly Speaker

**Answer:** (2) During the pleasure of the Governor
**Explanation:** Under Article 164(1), ministers hold office during the pleasure of the Governor. In practice, a minister must also retain the confidence of the CM and the Assembly.
**Article:** 164(1)

---

**Q35. A Bill which seeks to derogate from the powers of the High Court in a State:**
(1) Can be assented to by the Governor directly
(2) MUST be reserved by the Governor for the consideration of the President
(3) Cannot be introduced at all
(4) Requires a referendum

**Answer:** (2) MUST be reserved by the Governor for the consideration of the President
**Explanation:** Article 200 proviso: the Governor shall not assent to a Bill derogating from the powers of the High Court, but shall reserve it for the consideration of the President.
**Article:** 200 (proviso)

---

**Q36. According to Article 199, a Bill is a Money Bill if it deals ONLY with matters such as:**
(1) Imposition, abolition, or regulation of any tax
(2) All criminal law amendments
(3) Regulation of trade within the State
(4) Establishment of new districts

**Answer:** (1) Imposition, abolition, or regulation of any tax
**Explanation:** Article 199 lists matters for Money Bills: imposition/abolition/regulation of taxes, borrowing, Consolidated Fund custody, appropriation from the Fund, etc. The Speaker's certificate is conclusive on whether a Bill is a Money Bill.
**Article:** 199

---

**Q37. A Bill involving expenditure from the State's Consolidated Fund but not a Money Bill is called:**
(1) The Finance Bill
(2) An Ordinary Bill
(3) A Financial Bill (category II)
(4) A Constitutional Amendment Bill

**Answer:** (3) A Financial Bill (category II)
**Explanation:** Financial Bills which also involve expenditure from the Consolidated Fund cannot be introduced without the recommendation of the Governor, but are not Money Bills and can be introduced in either house.
**Article:** 207

---

**Q38. Money Bills and Financial Bills involving expenditure from the Consolidated Fund can be introduced in the State Legislature ONLY on the recommendation of:**
(1) The Chief Minister
(2) The Speaker
(3) The Governor
(4) The Finance Minister

**Answer:** (3) The Governor
**Explanation:** Under Articles 207(1) and 207(3), such Bills cannot be introduced except on the recommendation of the Governor. This mirrors Article 117 for the Union.
**Article:** 207

---

**Q39. Members of the State Legislature enjoy privileges under which Article?**
(1) Article 194
(2) Article 105
(3) Article 163
(4) Article 178

**Answer:** (1) Article 194
**Explanation:** Article 194 confers powers, privileges, and immunities on members of the State Legislature (freedom of speech in the House, immunity from court proceedings for statements made in the House, etc.). Article 105 is the corresponding provision for Parliament.
**Article:** 194

---

**Q40. The Speaker of the Legislative Assembly can be removed from office by a resolution passed by:**
(1) A simple majority of the Assembly
(2) A majority of the total membership, passed with 14 days' notice
(3) A two-thirds majority of the Assembly
(4) The Governor alone

**Answer:** (2) A majority of the total membership, passed with 14 days' notice
**Explanation:** Article 179(c) read with Article 178: the Speaker can be removed by a resolution of the Assembly passed by a majority of ALL the then members, after 14 days' notice. This is stricter than a simple majority of those present and voting.
**Article:** 179(c)

---

**Q41. The minimum number of members required to hold a meeting of the Legislative Assembly (quorum) is:**
(1) 1/3 of the total members
(2) 1/10 of the total members
(3) 1/2 of the total members
(4) 1/4 of the total members

**Answer:** (2) 1/10 of the total members
**Explanation:** Under Article 189(3), the quorum of the Legislative Assembly and Council is one-tenth of the total membership. For Karnataka's 224-member Assembly, that is 23 members.
**Article:** 189(3)

---

**Q42. Which of the following can declare a seat vacant if a member of the State Legislature is absent from all its meetings for more than 60 days without permission?**
(1) The Speaker
(2) The Governor
(3) The Chief Minister
(4) The Election Commission of India

**Answer:** (1) The Speaker
**Explanation:** Article 190(4): the House may declare a seat vacant if a member is absent from all meetings of the House for 60 days without permission. The presiding officer (Speaker/Chairman) initiates the process.
**Article:** 190(4)

---

**Q43. Which of the following statements about a Money Bill in a State is INCORRECT?**
(1) It can be introduced only in the Legislative Assembly
(2) The Legislative Council must return it within 14 days
(3) The Speaker's certificate that a Bill is a Money Bill is final
(4) The Legislative Council can amend a Money Bill

**Answer:** (4) The Legislative Council can amend a Money Bill
**Explanation:** The Legislative Council CANNOT amend a Money Bill; it can only make recommendations, which the Assembly may accept or reject. Statement (4) is incorrect — this is the answer.
**Article:** 198

---

**Q44. The annual State Budget is presented in the Legislature as:**
(1) The Finance Bill
(2) The Annual Financial Statement
(3) The Appropriation Bill
(4) The Supplementary Budget

**Answer:** (2) The Annual Financial Statement
**Explanation:** Article 202 requires the Governor to cause to be laid before the Legislature the Annual Financial Statement (the budget) for each financial year, showing estimated receipts and expenditure.
**Article:** 202

---

**Q45. No expenditure can be incurred from the State's Consolidated Fund except:**
(1) As authorized by the Governor's order
(2) As authorized by a law and by an Appropriation Act passed by the Legislature
(3) As authorized by the Chief Minister
(4) As recommended by the Finance Commission

**Answer:** (2) As authorized by a law and by an Appropriation Act passed by the Legislature
**Explanation:** Article 204: expenditure from the Consolidated Fund of the State requires authorization by law, and withdrawals are governed by an Appropriation Act. This gives the Legislature control over the purse.
**Article:** 204

---

**Q46. A 'vote on account' in a State Legislature enables:**
(1) Passing the full year's budget
(2) Withdrawing money from the Consolidated Fund before the full budget is passed
(3) Increasing taxes for the year
(4) Changing the financial year

**Answer:** (2) Withdrawing money from the Consolidated Fund before the full budget is passed
**Explanation:** Article 206 provides for votes on account — advance grant to cover expenditure for part of the year (e.g., first 2 months) until the Appropriation Act is passed.
**Article:** 206

---

**Q47. The Leader of the Opposition in the Legislative Assembly:**
(1) Is a constitutional post created by the Constitution
(2) Is recognized under the Salary and Allowances of Leaders of Opposition Act, 1977
(3) Is appointed by the Governor
(4) Is elected by the ruling party

**Answer:** (2) Is recognized under the Salary and Allowances of Leaders of Opposition in State Legislature Act, 1977
**Explanation:** The Leader of the Opposition in a State Legislature is recognized under the 1977 Act (a statutory post, not a constitutional one). He leads the largest opposition party in the House.
**Article:** Not in the Constitution (statutory)

**Q48. The Deputy Speaker of the Legislative Assembly can preside over the House when:**
(1) The Governor is absent
(2) The Speaker is absent or the office of Speaker is vacant
(3) The Chief Minister is absent
(4) The Assembly is dissolved

**Answer:** (2) The Speaker is absent or the office of Speaker is vacant
**Explanation:** Article 180: while the office of Speaker is vacant or when the Speaker is absent from any sitting, the Deputy Speaker performs the Speaker's duties. He also has the same powers regarding money bills, casting vote, etc.
**Article:** 180

---

**Q49. Which of the following is NOT a subject on which the State Legislature can make laws under the State List?**
(1) Police
(2) Public health
(3) Agriculture
(4) Defence

**Answer:** (4) Defence
**Explanation:** Defence is in the UNION List (Entry 1 of List I, Seventh Schedule). Police, public health, and agriculture are State List subjects. Hence Defence is NOT a State subject.
**Article:** Seventh Schedule, List I Entry 1

---

**Q50. Under Article 356, President's Rule in a State was imposed the MAXIMUM number of times in which State?**
(1) Kerala
(2) Punjab
(3) Jammu & Kashmir
(4) Bihar

**Answer:** (2) Punjab
**Explanation:** Historically, Punjab has seen President's Rule the most times (around 10 occasions). Kerala also saw it many times, but Punjab holds the record. Note: this is a frequently asked current-affairs/static GK fact.
**Article:** 356

### Advanced (10)

**Q51. In which landmark case did the Supreme Court hold that a State Legislative Assembly cannot claim absolute privilege from judicial review, and that Article 194 privileges must not violate Fundamental Rights?**
(1) Kesavananda Bharati v. State of Kerala
(2) Gunupati Keshavram Reddy v. Nafisul Hasan
(3) M.S.M. Sharma v. S.K. Sinha
(4) P.V. Narasimha Rao v. State

**Answer:** (2) Gunupati Keshavram Reddy v. Nafisul Hasan
**Explanation:** In Gunupati Keshavram Reddy (Keshav Singh case, 1965), the Allahabad High Court and then the Supreme Court held that the privileges of a State Legislature (Article 194) are subject to Fundamental Rights and are not absolute, and that courts can review breaches of fundamental rights by the Legislature. Option (3) is the famous 'Searchlight' case on freedom of speech vs. privilege.
**Article:** 194; Case: Keshav Singh (1965)

---

**Q52. The 'Searchlight' case (M.S.M. Sharma v. S.K. Sinha) on State Legislature privileges established that:**
(1) A member's freedom of speech in the House is absolute
(2) The court cannot interfere even if Fundamental Rights are violated
(3) Privileges are subject to Fundamental Rights, and the Speaker can prohibit publication of proceedings
(4) The Legislature can punish any citizen for contempt without trial

**Answer:** (3) Privileges are subject to Fundamental Rights, and the Speaker can prohibit publication of proceedings
**Explanation:** The Searchlight case (1959) held that Article 194 privileges are subject to Fundamental Rights (Article 19(1)(a)), but the Legislature can prohibit publication of its proceedings in the public interest, and such prohibition is binding. It also rejected a strict application of Article 20(3).
**Article:** 194; Case: M.S.M. Sharma (1959)

**Q53. Which Amendment Act added the provision that a defecting MLA/MLC cannot be appointed as a Minister (including CM/CM of a State) for a certain period?**
(1) 42nd Amendment
(2) 44th Amendment
(3) 91st Amendment
(4) 73rd Amendment

**Answer:** (3) 91st Amendment
**Explanation:** The 91st Amendment Act (2003) added Article 164(1B): a defector under the Tenth Schedule who is disqualified cannot be appointed as a Minister (including CM/Chief Minister) until re-elected. It also brought the 15% cap on ministry size.
**Article:** 164(1B); 91st Amendment, 2003

**Q54. An MLA who voluntarily gives up membership of his party is disqualified under which Schedule, and who decides on the disqualification?**
(1) Ninth Schedule; Governor
(2) Tenth Schedule; the Chairman/Speaker of the House
(3) Eleventh Schedule; Chief Minister
(4) Tenth Schedule; High Court

**Answer:** (2) Tenth Schedule; the Chairman/Speaker of the House
**Explanation:** The Tenth Schedule (Anti-Defection) disqualifies members who voluntarily give up party membership, vote against party directions, etc. The decision rests with the Speaker (Assembly) or Chairman (Council). This was upheld in Kihoto Hollohan v. Zachillhu (1992).
**Article:** Tenth Schedule; Case: Kihoto Hollohan

**Q55. The decision of the Speaker on a defection (disqualification) question is subject to judicial review. This was laid down in:**
(1) S.R. Bommai v. Union of India
(2) Kihoto Hollohan v. Zachillhu
(3) Minerva Mills v. Union of India
(4) Indira Nehru Gandhi v. Raj Narain

**Answer:** (2) Kihoto Hollohan v. Zachillhu
**Explanation:** Kihoto Hollohan (1992) upheld the Tenth Schedule's validity but struck down paragraph 7 (which had ousted judicial review). It held the Speaker's decision is subject to judicial review, though courts exercise limited scrutiny.
**Article:** Tenth Schedule; Case: Kihoto Hollohan (1992)

**Q56. When the Governor has reserved a Bill for the President and the President returns it for reconsideration, the State Legislature must reconsider it within:**
(1) 14 days
(2) 1 month
(3) 6 months
(4) No time limit is prescribed

**Answer:** (4) No time limit is prescribed
**Explanation:** Article 201 does not prescribe any time limit for the State Legislature to reconsider a Bill returned by the President. If the Bill is passed again, it is presented to the President again for assent.
**Article:** 201

**Q57. Which of the following is a correct statement about a Bill returned by the Governor for reconsideration and re-passed by the State Legislature?**
(1) The Governor can veto it a second time
(2) The Governor must give assent, but can reserve it for the President
(3) The Bill automatically lapses
(4) The Bill becomes an Act only after the President's assent

**Answer:** (2) The Governor must give assent, but can reserve it for the President
**Explanation:** Article 200: if the House passes the Bill again, the Governor MUST assent to it. However, the proviso allows the Governor to reserve it for the President where the Bill derogates from High Court powers. Unlike the President (Article 111), the Governor has no absolute veto over a re-passed Bill.
**Article:** 200

**Q58. Under the Doctrine of Pleasure (Article 156/164), the Governor's removal by the President is:**
(1) Subject to judicial review on any ground
(2) Not justiciable — no reasons need be given
(3) Requires the advice of the Prime Minister only
(4) Subject to the consent of the State Legislature

**Answer:** (2) Not justiciable — no reasons need be given
**Explanation:** The Governor holds office during the pleasure of the President (Article 156(1)), and no reasons need be given for removal (the pleasure is not justiciable). However, in B.P. Singhal v. Union of India (2010), the SC held the pleasure must be exercised on valid grounds, not arbitrarily.
**Article:** 156; Case: B.P. Singhal (2010)

**Q59. The 'double responsibility' of the Council of Ministers in a State means it is responsible to:**
(1) The Governor and the Assembly
(2) The Assembly and the people
(3) The Speaker and the Chief Minister
(4) The President and the Governor

**Answer:** (1) The Governor and the Assembly
**Explanation:** The Council of Ministers is collectively responsible to the Legislative Assembly (Article 164(2)), AND individually holds office during the pleasure of the Governor (Article 164(1)). This 'double responsibility' (to both the Assembly and the Governor) is a distinctive feature of State governments compared to the Union (where the President acts on advice, so responsibility is essentially to the Lok Sabha).
**Article:** 164(1) & 164(2)

**Q60. Which of the following is NOT a ground for the Supreme Court to review the imposition of President's Rule under Article 356?**
(1) The proclamation was based on malafide motives
(2) The proclamation was based on irrelevant or extraneous material
(3) The State has not been given an opportunity to be heard
(4) The Court can examine the facts leading to the proclamation

**Answer:** (4) The Court can examine the facts leading to the proclamation
**Explanation:** In S.R. Bommai v. Union of India (1994), the SC held that the validity of a proclamation under Article 356 is open to judicial review only on limited grounds: malafides, irrelevant/extraneous material, or absence of material. The Court does NOT examine the facts/merits — it only checks if there was ANY material. Statement (4) is therefore incorrect — it is the answer.
**Article:** 356; Case: S.R. Bommai (1994)
